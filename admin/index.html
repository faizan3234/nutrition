1 · Add labeled meal photo

Choose a clear photo. Label EACH visible dish separately, ideally with weighed edible grams and an annotation box. All foods will remain pending until you approve them.

Tap “Draw box” on an item, then drag across that food. A bounding box helps prepare object-detection training data, but it is not itself training.

Weight and nutrition must be entered independently. Do not derive verified protein values from photo appearance alone.

2 · Approve labeled examples

Only approved examples enter the reference index. Approval documents review—not laboratory validation or model accuracy.

3 · Approved references · retract mistakes

Deactivate incorrect references immediately. Historical user logs are retained; new corrected references require independent evidence and fresh approval.

4 · User correction queue

Corrections are untrusted until independently verified. Preview shared photos privately. Only photos with separate training/reference consent may be prepared as new references; nutrition still requires evidence and approval.

5 · What improves with admin data?

  • Exact uploaded reference photos recall approved foods and recorded serving grams.
  • Similar photos propose a recipe; the user must re-confirm weight.
  • Bounding boxes support subsequent detector training and evaluation.
  • Training manifests maintain grouped train/validation/test splits.
  • Measured camera accuracy and food-weight MAE remain unknown until held-out evaluations are actually run.

6 · Most useful corrections

Shows repeat problems and possible contradictions between approved recipes. Triage is advisory—no automatic edits.

7 · Validation laboratory

Dataset coverage and unvalidated AI metrics. Admin approvals alone cannot pass these gates.
